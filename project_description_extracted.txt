Abstract
Background. Segmentation models for CT are usually judged by overlap scores, which do not tell a radiologist whether a
whole lesion was missed. Distribution-free risk control (conformal risk control; Learn-then-Test) can certify error rates, but
published work mostly targets voxel- or organ-level risk, and a recent PET/CT study found that a single global probability
threshold may be unable to certify lesion-level missed-detection risk at all.
Objective. To determine what lesion-level risk can be certified for renal-mass detection on KiTS23, at what review burden,
with which prediction family, and whether the certificate survives natural acquisition shift and a change of reference
annotation.
Methods. nnU-Net v2 ensembles (residual-encoder M and L presets and the original 3d_fullres, with no architectural
changes) are trained on 60% of the 489 labelled KiTS23 cases, a threshold is calibrated on 20%, and 20% is held out for a
single evaluation (patient-level random partition). On the ensemble probability maps, six nested prediction families (single
model, ensemble mean, member maximum, mean plus β standard deviations, dilation, and component selection) are
certified against patient-level and lesion-level missed-detection risks using split-conformal critical thresholds, conformal risk
control and Learn-then-Test bounds. The primary efficiency endpoint is the mean number of false-positive lesions per patient
at matched certified risk. Two secondary modules study validity across acquisition cohort, slice thickness, histology and
tumour size, and sensitivity to the reference annotation using the repeated annotations of KiTS.
Analysis plan. The protocol is pre-registered. A Train-only Phase-0 gate fixes thresholds, families and the study framing
before Calibration or Test labels are read. The certification code is checked with planted faults, and inference uses
patient-level cluster bootstrap and paired non-parametric tests with multiplicity correction.
Expected contribution. A reproducible benchmark and an empirical map of when lesion-level certificates are feasible,
efficient and fragile in renal-mass CT. Null and negative findings are reported as findings. No clinical validity is claimed.
Results: not yet available (protocol stage).
Keywords: conformal risk control · lesion-level detection · renal mass · KiTS23 · nnU-Net · uncertainty quantification · distribution shift ·
inter-annotator variability

1.1 The problem
Automatic segmentation of kidney tumours is usually reported with overlap scores such as Dice. A good average Dice can hide
the failure that matters most in practice: a whole lesion the model never flagged. A tool that acts as a second reader needs a
different kind of statement. Not “the model is usually accurate”, but “for a new patient, the chance that at least one lesion is
missed is at most α”. Statements of this kind are risk guarantees, and producing them is the subject of conformal risk control.
1.2 The idea
For every CT scan the models produce a probability map. A review region is obtained by flagging every voxel whose score
passes a threshold: the more permissive the threshold, the larger the region a human has to look at. Calibration patients,
whom the models never saw in training, show how permissive the threshold must be for lesions to be covered. Conformal risk
control (CRC) and Learn-then-Test (LTT) turn that observation into a distribution-free guarantee. It does not require the
model’s probabilities to be well calibrated, only that a new patient is exchangeable with the calibration patients.
Three things can break such a certificate, and the project studies each of them:
Failure mode
What happens
How the project studies it
Floor
Some lesions are not supported by the model at any
threshold, so no threshold can reach the target risk. A 2026
PET/CT study reported this for a single global threshold.
Compare prediction families that use ensemble
disagreement and candidate components; report the
floor explicitly.
Shift
If new patients differ from the calibration patients (contrast
phase, slice thickness, histology, tumour size), the guarantee
can fail.
Measure realized risk across natural subgroups and
cohorts inside KiTS23; test group-conditional calibration.
Reference
The guarantee is relative to the annotation that defines “a
lesion”. KiTS provides repeated annotations.
Compare risk against the released label, each annotation
repeat, and their union and intersection.

1.4 Key terms
Term
Meaning in this project
Lesion
One renal mass (tumour or cyst), taken from the released instance mask if present, otherwise a 26-connected
component of the tumour ∪ cyst mask.
Prediction family
A nested family of flagged regions Fλ indexed by one parameter λ. Larger λ flags more.
Detected lesion
A lesion whose voxels are covered by Fλ at fraction ≥ γ (γ = 0.5 primary; 0.1 and 0.9 as sensitivity analyses).
Rpatient
Patient-level risk: 1 if any lesion of the patient is missed, else 0.
Rlesion
Lesion-level risk: the per-patient mean of lesion-miss indicators (macro average).
Critical λ
For a patient, the smallest λ at which all of its lesions are detected (∞ if none).
Floor
Share of calibration patients that still have a missed lesion at the most permissive grid point. Certification at level
α is impossible if it exceeds α.
Certificate
A statement such as P(no missed lesion) ≥ 1 − α, or E[Rlesion] ≤ α, proved under exchangeability.
CRC / LTT
Conformal risk control (expected-risk guarantee) and Learn-then-Test / RCPS (high-probability guarantee).
Review burden
Mean number of false-positive lesions per patient (primary) and flagged volume outside lesions (secondary).

What the literature changed in the design
• Voxel-level conformal methods are crowded, so the unit of risk is the lesion and the patient.
• A global threshold can fail outright, so the study compares prediction families and reports the floor.
• Guarantees break under shift, so a natural-shift and subgroup module is included, with group-conditional remedies.
• Strong CNN baselines leave little room for architecture gains, so nnU-Net is unchanged and ensembles serve uncertainty
only.
• Significance testing is rare in segmentation papers (an audit of MICCAI 2023 found about 10% [A]), so the analysis is
pre-registered, paired and multiplicity-corrected.

4.2 Cohort, labels and annotation repeats
• Tumour-positive surgical or ablation cohort. Every patient has a tumour, so patient-level false-alarm risk (specificity)
cannot be estimated. All claims apply to lesion-positive populations only.
• Heterogeneity that makes shift testable. Two contrast phases, a tenfold range of slice thickness, several histologic
subtypes and a wide range of tumour sizes.
• Lesions are instances of tumour ∪ cyst. Cysts therefore count as lesions to detect. Tumour-only detection is a secondary
analysis, because treating cysts as false positives inflates false-positive counts (as reported for a tumour-only nnU-Net
baseline on KiTS23 [R]).
• Annotation provenance is a multi-annotator process whose details should be confirmed from the KiTS21 and KiTS23
papers before the manuscript states them. The drafts describe it as trainee annotation with expert review, which I did not
verify.
4.3 Splits and phases
All 489 labelled cases are partitioned once, at patient level and by simple random assignment (stratified splitting would break
exchangeability between calibration and test patients): Train 60% (≈ 293), Calibration 20% (≈ 98), Test 20% (≈ 98). The seed is
fixed and the partition hash is logged. Balance checks cover cohort, tumour size, slice thickness and cysts; if the pre-registered
balance rule fails, the next seed is used and every draw is logged. Calibration and Test labels stay unread until Phase 0 closes.

5 Architecture
5.1 System architecture
The system has four layers. The data layer partitions KiTS23 at patient level. The model layer trains an nnU-Net v2 pool on the
Train split only. The prediction layer turns member softmax maps into per-voxel scores and six nested prediction families. The
certification and evaluation layer calibrates a threshold on the Calibration split, evaluates realized risk and review burden on
the Test split, and feeds the shift (B) and annotation-repeat (C) modules.

5.2 Model architecture: nnU-Net v2 residual-encoder U-Net
The project deliberately does not change the network. nnU-Net v2 configures preprocessing, network topology, patch size
and batch size from a dataset fingerprint. Its default 3D full-resolution U-Net uses instance normalisation, leaky ReLU (slope
0.01), two convolutional blocks per resolution step in encoder and decoder, and deep supervision on all but the two lowest
resolutions. Training minimises Dice plus cross-entropy with SGD (Nesterov momentum 0.99, initial learning rate 0.01,
polynomial decay) for 1000 epochs of 250 iterations [A].
Residual-encoder (ResEnc) variants use residual blocks in the encoder, keep a conventional decoder, and, in the M, L and XL
presets, scale batch and patch size to a VRAM budget (roughly 9–11 GB, 24 GB and 40 GB or more) [A]. In nnU-Net Revisited,
on KiTS fold 0, the original configuration reached Dice 86.25 in 8.9 A100 GPU-hours, ResEnc-M 87.91 in 11.4 and ResEnc-L
88.60 in 35.3 [R].

5.3 Prediction families
A prediction family is a nested set of flagged regions Fλ. Probability-type families flag a voxel when its score is at least 1 − λ,
with thresholds on a pre-registered log-spaced grid from 0.5 down to 10−6; the dilation family uses a radius grid in
millimetres. Larger λ always means a larger flagged set, which makes every loss non-increasing in λ.

Procedure
1. For each calibration patient and family, evaluate the nested sets Fλ on the λ grid.
2. For each lesion, compute the coverage c = |lesion ∩ Fλ| / |lesion|; the lesion is detected if c ≥ γ.
3. Record each patient’s critical λ (∞ if some lesion is never detected) and its lesion-level loss curve.
4. Compute λ* by the split-conformal rule (patient level), the CRC rule (lesion level) and LTT (high probability). Declare the
family infeasible if a rule cannot be met.
5. Apply λ* to every Test patient and record realized risk, false-positive lesions and flagged volume. Repeat on 1,000 random
re-splits of the Calibration ∪ Test pool to show how realized risk varies (descriptive only).
Assumptions checked for each theorem: exchangeable patients (by random partition); loss non-increasing in λ
(property-tested on every patient’s curve); loss bounded in [0, 1]. Not checked: independence of lesions within a patient,
which is why risks are macro-averaged per patient.
5.5 Module B: shift and subgroups
Natural variables inside KiTS23: acquisition cohort (original corticomedullary KiTS21 cases vs newly added cases that include
the nephrogenic phase, verified with contrast statistics), slice thickness, histologic subtype and tumour-size stratum (≤ 4, 4–7,
> 7 cm). Experiments:
• subgroup-conditional realized risk under pooled calibration;
• cohort-to-cohort transfer of λ* (the models have seen both cohorts, so this is calibration shift, not model shift);
• remedies: group-conditional (Mondrian) calibration and weighted conformal prediction, reporting the burden cost.
These analyses are confirmatory only when each compared group has at least 30 patients in both Calibration and Test;
otherwise they are reported as exploratory, with effect sizes and intervals.
5.6 Module C: annotation repeats
KiTS21 collected three separate annotations per region of interest, and a third-party catalogue says KiTS23 keeps
per-annotation masks per case. Analyses, run on every case that has repeats:
• lesion-level agreement and Dice across repeats;
• realized risk of the threshold calibrated on the released label, measured against each repeat, the union and the
intersection;
• the threshold calibrated on the union, and its burden cost;
• Spearman correlation (bootstrap interval; partial for lesion volume) between ensemble disagreement and repeat
disagreement per lesion.
Label-noise theory is cited, not applied, until its conditions are checked against this noise structure. Module C is dropped, with
a recorded note, if repeats exist for fewer than 150 cases.
5.7 Metrics and statistics
• Segmentation: official KiTS23 code (Dice and surface Dice on the three hierarchical classes) to show baseline competence.
• Detection: lesion sensitivity and false positives by size stratum (unit-tested matching).
• Calibration: Brier score, NLL, class-wise ECE (flagged as imbalance-sensitive) and lesion-level reliability.
• Secondary failure detection: risk–coverage and AURC with ensemble pairwise Dice as the baseline.
• Inference: patient-level cluster-bootstrap intervals; paired Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction over at most 8
pre-specified comparisons; Clopper–Pearson intervals for rates; 5 seeds with their spread; 1,000 re-splits shown
descriptively (re-splits are not independent, so no p-values come from them).
